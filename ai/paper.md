# On knot detection via picture recognition

Anne Dranowski, Yura Kabkov and Daniel Tubbenhauer study which information a machine can extract from a knot picture before symbolic topology enters. The three questions are to count visible crossings, recover the combinatorial diagram including overpasses, and identify the knot. A single curl separates the visible count from the invariant minimal crossing number. [Main article in Markdown](main-article.md) · [Paper on arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.06284)

For a regular diagram whose underpasses are independently gapped or filled, the crossing count is `e - b + f`. Here `e` counts free ink ends, `b` counts connected pieces of ink, and `f` counts bounded complementary faces. With all underpasses gapped, cutting a single circle at its positive number of underpasses gives that many arcs. A raster can join nearby pieces, break an arc, or introduce an extra tip after thinning. [Rendered comparison](../figures/poster-transfer.pdf)

The image archive has 9,051 related views. A fixed four-feature nearest-neighbor classifier recovers almost every label on one random image split. The matched test holds the training set fixed and compares 1,054 familiar-source views with 1,054 views from omitted sources. The test arms have identical crossing-label and scale distributions and disjoint PNG hashes. The new CNN uses the same test construction. A separate two-strand braid experiment controls the diagram under a single Reidemeister-I move and provides known crossing locations through geometric intersection enumeration.
